Direct-manipulation: the deformed cross-section IS the interface. Click the cylinder, the clamp body, or a bolt — the drawer opens on that part's parameters and live numbers. Drag the torque slider and watch the ears bend, the tube go oval, and the gap close. Every part is painted with its own safety factor.
click a part to edit it · colors: green ≥2 · amber ≥1.2 · red <1.2
A thin tube ovalizes and crushes — often the real limit, not the clamp. Solid rods barely care.
The ears are little cantilevers: bolt offset e is their lever arm, tf² their strength. The gap is the deflection budget.
Torque lives on the slider under the section. K scatters ±25% — torque is a fuzzy proxy for preload.
Grip is friction only — never key on friction alone for safety-critical torque; add a keyway or pin if slipping hurts.

What the exaggerated deformation is telling you: the ears rotating toward each other and the cylinder going elliptical are the two motions that consume the flange gap.
