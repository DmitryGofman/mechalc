HOW IT WORKS — THE DIMENSIONS

One height dimension. The body is a flat block, so you set H — its height above the split face — and the two bending sections follow from it. The ear section at the bolt is the full H. The crown section, the material left over the bore, is whatever is above the hole: tc = gap/2 + H − D/2. The crown is always the thinner of the two, which is why these clamps crack over the bore and not at the bolts. Nothing here can jump: change the gap and tc shifts by that same half-millimetre, because the block really did move up by it.

THE EQUATIONS

Where the constants come from. The nut factor K lumps thread and under-head friction (~90% of your wrench effort); it scatters ±25% between real joints, so treat preload as a band. η ≈ 0.75 derates the friction grip because a real bore never touches uniformly. λ = 0.2 is the share of clamp load treated as a non-uniform pinch that ovalizes a tube rather than pure hoop compression. Creep retention (45–60% for printed polymers) is a typical stress-relaxation figure, not a measured one for your print settings. Calibrate against one bench pull-test before trusting absolute numbers.

What you're watching: deflections are real but tiny — SPLIT × magnifies the flange gap and every deflection by the same factor, so the halves close in true proportion and meet exactly when the joint bottoms out. Ears rotate down toward the split plane, the tube squashes vertically and bulges sideways, and the flange gap closes; when it shuts, the halves touch and grip stops growing no matter how hard you pull. With few bolts on a wide clamp you'll also see the halves lift between bolts — that's uneven grip. Typical reference values; verify before production.
