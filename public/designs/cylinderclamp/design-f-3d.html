HOW IT WORKS — THE DIMENSIONS

One height dimension. The body is a flat block, so you set H — its height above the split face — and the two bending sections follow from it. The ear section at the bolt is the full H. The crown section, the material left over the bore, is whatever is above the hole: tc = gap/2 + H − D/2. The crown is always the thinner of the two, which is why these clamps crack over the bore and not at the bolts. Nothing here can jump: change the gap and tc shifts by that same half-millimetre, because the block really did move up by it.

Why the crown carries more moment than the ear

What the duty inputs mean

Everything a clamp does comes down to one question: will it slip on the cylinder? The two duty inputs are the service loads trying to make it slip — neither has anything to do with bolt torque. Slides along axis is a straight pull or push along the cylinder, in newtons: the weight it carries, a belt tension, a cable pulling on it. Twists around axis is a moment trying to rotate the clamp about the cylinder, in N·m: a lever hanging off it, a reaction torque. Grip safety factor is how much margin you want over that duty — 2 means the joint should hold twice what you expect.

They are combined by converting the twist into the equivalent axial force it puts on the friction interface, Feq = 2Tduty/D, then adding the two. That is conservative — it assumes the worst case where both act at once and in the same sense. The grip that resists them is friction only, Fax = η·μ·π·ΣF, checked after creep has relaxed the preload. Friction alone should never be the sole safeguard on something that hurts when it slips — add a key, a pin or a shoulder.

Why so much of the part sits near zero

Why the outer ends of the flanges carry nothing. Past the bolt line there is no load left to carry — the material outboard of the bolt is an overhang, so its bending moment is zero and it colours neutral. It is not a modelling gap: that material is there to hold the bolt head and to give you something to grip, not to carry bending. Between the bore wall and the bolt the moment ramps up as M = F·(a − |z|), and it peaks over the bore where the section is thinnest. If you want the ears to work harder, move the bolt closer to the bore (smaller e) — that shortens the lever on the crown too.

CALCULATION REPORT

Where the constants come from. The nut factor K lumps thread and under-head friction (~90% of your wrench effort); it scatters ±25% between real joints, so treat preload as a band. η ≈ 0.75 derates the friction grip because a real bore never touches uniformly. λ = 0.2 is the share of clamp load treated as a non-uniform pinch that ovalizes a tube rather than pure hoop compression. Creep retention (45–60% for printed polymers) is a typical stress-relaxation figure, not a measured one for your print settings. Calibrate against one bench pull-test before trusting absolute numbers.

DESIGNING A TWO-PIECE CLAMP — WHAT ACTUALLY MATTERS
What you're watching: deflections are real but tiny — SPLIT × magnifies the flange gap and every deflection by the same factor, so the halves close in true proportion and meet exactly when the joint bottoms out. Ears rotate down toward the split plane, the tube squashes vertically and bulges sideways, and the flange gap closes; when it shuts, the halves touch and grip stops growing no matter how hard you pull. With few bolts on a wide clamp you'll also see the halves lift between bolts — that's uneven grip. Typical reference values; verify before production.
