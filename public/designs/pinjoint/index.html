THE SHARED MODEL — EVERY WAY THIS JOINT CAN FAIL (SHIGLEY FIG 8-23)
applied load F ─► PIN: shear τ = F/(n·A) n = 1 (lap) or 2 (clevis) planes │ bending σ = 32M/πd³ (clevis: M = F/2·(t₂/4 + gap + t₁/2)) │ bearing on the pin surface p = Fᵢ/(d·t) │ └► PLATES (each, with its own material and share of F): bearing on the hole wall σ = Fᵢ/(d·t) crushes the hole net-section tension σ = Fᵢ/((w−d)·t) tears across the hole edge tear-out τ = Fᵢ/(2·t·(a−d/2)) shears the margin out capacity of the joint = the smallest of all of them — everything is linear in F

The failure modes checked everywhere — straight from Shigley's Fig 8-23: (c) shear of the pin across one or two planes vs 0.577·Sy; (b) bending of the pin as a tiny clevis beam; (e) bearing of pin on members and members on pin over the projected area t·d; (d) tensile failure across the hole's net section (Eq 8-54); and (f)/(g) edge shear tear-out of the margin (Fig 8-25) on the two ligaments 2·t·(a−d/2). Structural practice avoids tear-out by keeping the hole at least 1.5 diameters from the edge — the calculator warns when you don't.

Two or three flanges: with three plates the load splits symmetrically — two shear planes, outer plates each carry F/2 — and the pin bends like a simply-supported beam. With two plates there is one shear plane, both plates carry the full F, and the offset load path tilts the joint (flagged, not modelled). A bolt option swaps the full shank circle for the ~75% minor-diameter core when threads sit in the shear plane.

Materials: pins from mild steel to hardened dowel, bolt classes 8.8/10.9, stainless, titanium, aluminum, brass; plates in steels, stainless, aluminum, brass, FR-4 and printed plastics (in-plane values, with orientation and creep warnings). Static checks against yield onset — no stress concentration (matters for fatigue), no preload friction. All typical reference values — verify before production use.
