Wire-Gate Clip Spring

Two interaction prototypes for one calculator: the bent-wire gate spring on wire-gate carabiners, snap-hook clasps and spring buckle hooks — a U-loop of spring wire whose own flex is the spring. Same physics engine under both (wiregate-engine.js) — what differs is how you drive it.

DESIGN B

Swing the Gate

A plan view of the clip you can actually operate: grab the nose and swing it about the pivot pin. The rotation is rigid-body; the offset pin drags the loop into its spread bow, colour runs pin-cool to apex-hot, a gauge follows your finger, and past the yield arc the gate hangs open. Release and the preload snaps it shut.

The model both prototypes share

The mechanism, as the hardware actually builds it: the two bent tips sit as pins in holes — they rotate freely, so the wire's ends carry no moment. The gate's swing is rigid-body rotation about the long-leg pin. The spring action is geometric: the second pin sits a distance a from the pivot, so a swing of φ tries to move one pinned end relative to the other by the chord 2a·sin(φ/2); both pins are held, so the loop absorbs the mismatch by flexing in its own plane — every part of the wire moves a little. Assembly already imposes a preload spread δ₀ through the offset holes (US 4,423,757), and that is what snaps the gate shut.

With pinned ends the moment is F·x from each pin: zero at the tangs, maximum at the U-bend — so the stress check lives at the apex, on curved wire, with the torsion-spring inner-fibre factor Ki (Shigley 10-43, C = w/d). The loop's spread compliance comes from Castigliano over the full developed length, which is why the rate is a few N/mm where a clamped leg would fight at ten times that.

Scope: static, frictionless pins, in-plane bending, small swing; the swing-to-spread chord is a worst case — measure δ₀ and the real spread on the part when it matters.
